## PREAMBLE — what this project is
rmxOS is an **open-source operating system** — a Darwin/Mach userland ported onto stock FreeBSD 15, developed in the open like FreeBSD or illumos. This is a **maintainer's own code-quality check of ONE feature of our service manager (launchd)** — how it runs a job on a schedule: every N seconds, or at calendar times like "02:30 daily," the way cron would. Ordinary pre-release engineering on our own source — the goal is simply to confirm this one path is well-built for our own users.

## THE ONE FEATURE (this is the whole scope)
launchd's **timed/scheduled launch** in `core.c`: the `StartInterval` periodic timer (`j->start_interval`, armed via `EVFILT_TIMER` at `:2898-2900`) and the `StartCalendarInterval` calendar schedule (`struct calendarinterval` `:268`, `calendarinterval_setalarm` `:282`, `calendarinterval_callback` `:283`, the global `sorted_calendar_events` list `:276`). launchd computes when a job is next due, sleeps on a timer, launches the job when it fires, and re-arms for the next occurrence. op-264 read KeepAlive/restart on this same daemon core (and only *deleted* these timers on job-remove at `:1600-1608`); this reads the scheduling logic itself. Confirm this one timed-launch path fires jobs at the right time — nothing wider.

## CONTEXT
Scheduled launch is launchd's cron role: a job asks to run periodically or at wall-clock times, and launchd must fire it at the right moment, exactly once per due time, and re-arm cleanly for the next. The failure shapes are the classic scheduler ones — drift, a missed re-arm that stops the schedule, a double-fire, or calendar math that lands on the wrong instant across a month/day/DST rollover. This path drives periodic maintenance jobs and complements op-264's keep-it-running supervision. Plain software-engineering framing: interval re-arm, calendar next-time computation, multi-entry ordering, and timer lifetime on stop/remove.

## THE QUESTIONS (all about this one path)
1. **Interval re-arm.** Does a `StartInterval=N` job fire on the N-second cadence and re-arm each time — no drift into a hot re-fire, no lost re-arm that silently stops the schedule? If a run is still in progress when the interval elapses, does the documented behavior (skip vs. pend) hold rather than stacking overlapping launches?
2. **Calendar next-time computation.** Does `StartCalendarInterval` compute the next matching wall-clock time correctly across the fields (minute / hour / day / weekday / month), including end-of-hour / end-of-day / end-of-month rollover, and does `setalarm` re-arm for the following occurrence after each fire — landing on the intended instant, not one period early/late?
3. **Multiple entries + global ordering.** For a job with several calendar entries, is each armed, and does the global `sorted_calendar_events` ordering fire the earliest-due entry correctly and re-sort after each fire — no entry starved, none fired twice for one due time?
4. **Timer lifetime on stop/remove.** When a scheduled job is stopped or removed, are its interval timer AND its calendar entries cancelled and freed cleanly (`calendarinterval_delete`, the sorted-list unlink), with no stale alarm left that would later fire into a removed job? (op-264 confirmed the `:1600-1608` delete of `start_interval`/`exit_timeout`; this extends the check to the calendar entries + the global list.)
