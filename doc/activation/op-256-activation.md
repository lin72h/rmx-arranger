## CONTEXT (read first — engineering framing)
Open-source OS engineering — an API-surface design consult on OUR OWN in-tree thread-pool seam (a NextBSD/Darwin userland ported onto stock FreeBSD 15/stable). Ordinary runtime/OS design: how our libdispatch consumes its kernel-managed thread pool, and what shape that seam should take. No target, no adversary, no exploitation — the same class of work as designing GCD's workqueue interface. This is the benign design half of the same subsystem op-255 reviews for correctness; split out so the two run independently.

## WHY (one line)
We carry the Darwin `pthread_workqueue` SPI verbatim (`include/pthread/workqueue_private.h`, SPI_VERSION 20170201; kernel `sys/kern/kern_thrworkq.c`; userspace `lib/libthr/thread/thr_workq.c`) and libdispatch already consumes it as its executor — but the SPI is the 2017 private surface. The narrow question: **what is the right EXPOSURE shape for libdispatch's pthread_workqueue seam** — keep the raw 2017 addthreads/should_narrow private SPI, or mimic whatever cleaner PUBLIC/SPI surface macOS 27 now ships for the same "libdispatch ↔ kernel-managed thread pool" relationship.

## SCOPE — the exposure design (NOT the governor)
1. **The current libdispatch↔workqueue seam.** Characterize how our libdispatch consumes the pool today (`_pthread_workqueue_addthreads`, `_pthread_workqueue_should_narrow(priority)`, the QoS-override family) and which parts are load-bearing for libdispatch vs vestigial 2017 surface.
2. **macOS-27 mimic target (op-252 recon FOLDED IN — Arranger-verified, findings are hypotheses to source-confirm).** op-252 mapped macOS 27's thread-pool seam: Apple did NOT replace the pthread_workqueue SPI — it kept the 2017-era SPI as the INTERNAL mechanism (19 `_pthread_workqueue_*` still exported in `libsystem_pthread.tbd`; **our ported SPI_VERSION 20170201 baseline matches** — F1) and layered a PUBLIC width-sizing face on top (`os_workgroup_parallel` + `os_workgroup_max_parallel_threads`, workgroup_object.h:329-336 — F2). GCD/libdispatch on macOS consumes the SPI DIRECTLY (F4: GCD = SPI-level; os_workgroup is the public face for foreign runtimes like Swift concurrency, NOT libdispatch's own path). **Implication (libdispatch exposure, non-governor):** the recon points toward KEEP — our 2017 SPI is ABI-current and is exactly the shape libdispatch itself uses on macOS, so libdispatch's workqueue seam stays the INTERNAL SPI, not a re-expressed public API. The public `os_workgroup` face is the GOVERNOR concern (foreign runtimes) → op-251 (deferred), out of scope here. Oracle: confirm/refute this keep recommendation against our libdispatch's actual consumption + cite; flag any 2017-vs-macOS-27 SPI drift that would make our ported seam diverge from what libdispatch expects.
3. **Exposure boundary for iteration 1.** What of the workqueue SPI should be exposed as a stable-ish seam vs kept internal/unstable — for LIBDISPATCH'S needs only. (The broader "expose to foreign runtimes as a governor" question is op-251, deferred — do not answer it here; just keep the libdispatch-facing surface clean and don't foreclose it.)
