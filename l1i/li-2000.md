## What this instruction means

openclaw is a **"Multi-channel AI gateway with extensible messaging integrations"** (`nx/openclaw`, `name:
openclaw`, `version: 2026.4.6`, MIT) — a large Node/TypeScript **monolith** built as a pnpm workspace:
- a **plugin-sdk** (`./plugin-sdk`, `/core`, `/provider-setup`, `/sandbox`, `/self-hosted-provider-setup`) —
  an extensible provider/plugin surface;
- native app shells under `apps/{macos,ios,android,shared}`;
- channel packages under `packages/` (clawdbot, moltbot, memory-host-sdk, plugin-package-contract) and
  `extensions/*`; messaging integrations (discord/matrix/baileys/…);
- heavyweight native deps (node-pty, node-llama-cpp, sharp, @napi-rs/canvas, matrix crypto, koffi);
- a **Sparkle `appcast.xml`** (macOS auto-update) and Docker sandbox images.

It is the first application big and real enough to prove rmxOS as a *product platform*, not just a userland
port. The port project lives in `~/wip-openclaw/` (upstream mirror `~/wip-openclaw/nx/openclaw`; the current
integration agent is `~/wip-openclaw/wip-codex54x`).

## The integration thesis — three stacked strategies (Coordinator-set precedence, Oracle to extend)

- **strategy-A — enable openclaw's macOS optimizations on rmxOS where they make sense.** openclaw already
  carries Darwin/macOS integration (launchd/LaunchAgent lifecycle, the Sparkle updater, `apps/macos`); rmxOS is
  a Darwin userland, so those paths can light up natively instead of being stubbed to a generic-Unix fallback.
- **strategy-B — break the monolith into small on-demand processes.** Split the single long-running Node
  process along its natural seams (channels, plugins, providers) into small processes that launchd loads and
  unloads **on demand**, so idle capacity costs no memory and the footprint tracks actual use.
- **strategy-C — (built on B) rewrite the hot small processes in Swift** so they ride our GCD /
  `pthread_workqueue` **dynamic concurrency** (kernel-managed width, no thread explosion) plus other rmxOS-unique
  runtime features. The Node↔Swift boundary and which processes are worth rewriting is the design call.

The Oracle is asked to find the optimization points across A/B/C and to **add further strategy categories** if
the architecture warrants (e.g. a caching/state-sharing plane, a native-dep substitution plane).

## Substrate this leans on (already-scoped ops)
- **launchd on-demand load/unload** — the socket/demand-activation launch path (op-268, held for reframe) is
  the mechanism strategy-B's on-demand processes ride; **pre-exec setup** (op-275) configures each child.
- **libxpc IPC** (li-007) — the transport between the split small processes.
- **pthread_workqueue governor** (li-1000, op-265) — the dynamic-concurrency signal strategy-C's Swift
  processes size themselves from, so many small processes share one load-aware core budget.
- **the immutable/ZFS updater** (op-274) — where openclaw's Sparkle-style update maps onto our platform updater.
